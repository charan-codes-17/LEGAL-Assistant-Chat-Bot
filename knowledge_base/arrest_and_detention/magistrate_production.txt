[TITLE: CrPC Section 57 & Section 167 / Article 22(2) — Mandatory Production Before Magistrate Within 24 Hours]
[AUTHORITY: Ministry of Law and Justice, Government of India]
[URL: https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/c9fe9c9b6840524844316f74bb1c556c.pdf]

STATUTORY PROVISIONS ON 24-HOUR CUSTODY LIMIT:
1. CrPC Section 57 (BNSS Section 58): Person arrested not to be detained more than twenty-four hours.
   "No police officer shall detain in custody a person arrested without warrant for a longer period than under all the circumstances of the case is reasonable, and such period shall not, in the absence of a special order of a Magistrate under section 167, exceed twenty-four hours exclusive of the time necessary for the journey from the place of arrest to the Magistrate's Court."

2. CrPC Section 167 (BNSS Section 187): Procedure when investigation cannot be completed in twenty-four hours.
   - If investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours and there are grounds for believing the accusation is well-founded, the officer in charge must forward the accused to the nearest Judicial Magistrate, together with a copy of the case diary entries.
   - Only a judicial magistrate can authorize further detention (police custody or judicial custody).

CONSEQUENCES OF VIOLATION (FAILURE TO PRODUCE WITHIN 24 HOURS):
1. The detention becomes illegal and unconstitutional from the 25th hour onward, constituting a violation of Article 21 and Article 22(2).
2. The detaining police officer is guilty of wrongful confinement under the Indian Penal Code (IPC Section 342 / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita Section 127).
3. The detainee or their relatives are entitled to immediately invoke:
   - Habeas Corpus writ petition before the High Court (Art. 226) or Supreme Court (Art. 32).
   - Application before the Chief Judicial Magistrate / Sessions Judge for immediate release.
   - Constitutional tort compensation against the State for illegal detention (Supreme Court in State of Maharashtra v. Christian Community Welfare Council, and Bhim Singh v. State of J&K).
